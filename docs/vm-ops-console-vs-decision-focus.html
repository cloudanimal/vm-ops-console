VM Ops Console vs Decision Focus

They look like competitors on paper, but they live on different floors of the building. Decision Focus is a GRC governance platform; the VM Ops Console is an operational vulnerability-management workbench. Here's how they actually line up — and why they're more complementary than rival.

Three layers, three jobs

Govern
Decision Focus (ISMS module) — risk register, control & framework mapping, board-ready posture. Consumes summaries, reports up.
Operate
VM Ops Console — triage findings, prioritize, assign, run remediation campaigns, track SLAs. Works the CVE-level reality.
Detect
Tenable.io (and peers) — scans assets, detects CVEs, scores them. The source the console reads.

Data flows up: scanners detect → the console operationalizes → a GRC platform governs. Decision Focus sits at the top; the console sits in the middle.

What each one is

Govern

Decision Focus

No-code GRC platform

Danish SaaS GRC suite (est. 2004, ~65 staff, acquired by Keensight Capital 2025). 20+ configurable modules; security lives in an ISMS module. Ingests posture data, maps it to frameworks, reports to committees. Does not scan.

Operate

VM Ops Console

Browser-local VM workbench

Static SPA — findings triage, VPR/EPSS/KEV prioritization, owners, SLAs, remediation campaigns, agent-coverage, PowerShell remediation samples. Reads Tenable.io data; 100% in your browser.

Detect

Tenable.io (reference)

Vulnerability scanner

Discovers assets, detects CVEs, computes VPR/CVSS/EPSS. The detection engine underneath — shown here as the source layer, not a rival to either.

The bottom line

They don't really compete — they'd stack. Decision Focus governs risk top-down for compliance and the board; the console operates vulnerabilities bottom-up at the CVE level. If an org ran Decision Focus for GRC, something like the console (or Tenable itself) would still be needed to do the actual triage-and-fix work, and would feed posture up into it.

What the console does that Decision Focus doesn't:

  • Works the findings, not the roll-up — per-CVE triage, VPR/EPSS/KEV prioritization, owners, dated status log.
  • Operational SLA + campaigns — remediation campaigns, SLA windows, overdue flags, MTTR — the day-to-day of a VM program.
  • Tenable-native — it reads the scanners you actually run. Notably, Decision Focus's security integrations are BitSight / BigFix / QRadar — not Tenable, Qualys, or Rapid7.
  • Data stays local — 100% in-browser; nothing uploaded to a vendor cloud.

What Decision Focus does that the console doesn't (and shouldn't try to):

  • Framework & control mapping — ISO 27001 / NIST / NIS2 / DORA compliance, its core strength.
  • Enterprise GRC breadth — 20+ modules (ERM, TPRM, audit, DORA, regulatory reporting) unifying risk across the whole org.
  • Board-facing governance — committee-ready posture across every risk domain, not just vulns.
